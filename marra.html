05 / Process & payment

Know what happens next—and what you’re paying for.

A clear process.
An open conversation.

Our first conversation can be completely open. We’ll talk about what you need, whether design is part of it, and how much help makes sense before putting a scope and fee in writing.

This phased design service is a new part of Marra. We’re bringing our construction and architectural experience together, and we’re eager to explore ideas with you through a direct, collaborative working relationship.

For design projects.

An example of how a design fee can be divided

SD

Schematic design

Establish the brief, review the site, and explore layouts, building form, and an initial budget direction. We compare options and agree on a direction before developing it further.

20% of fee
DD

Design development

Refine the chosen scheme: dimensions, materials, windows, major systems, and coordination with the project’s consultants. We revisit the budget as decisions become more specific.

20% of fee
CD

Construction documents

Develop the drawings, details, and specifications needed for pricing, permit review, and construction, with engineering and other specialist input as required.

40% of fee
PR

Permit & pricing coordination

Coordinate the submission, respond to review comments within the agreed scope, and help clarify contractor pricing.

5% of fee
CA

Construction administration

Provide agreed design support during construction: answer drawing questions, review relevant submittals, make scheduled site visits, and help resolve design details as work progresses.

15% of fee

These percentages illustrate a possible allocation of the total design fee—not a percentage of construction cost or a fixed rate for every project. We agree on the actual fee, phase scope, invoicing schedule, and any retainer before starting. Consultant fees, surveys, permit fees, extra revisions, and other exclusions are identified in the proposal. Construction is priced separately.

For construction projects.

A simpler arrangement where the scope allows

50%

Before work begins.

For suitable smaller projects, half of the agreed construction price is paid up front to arrange materials, scheduling, and mobilization.

50%

When the work is finished.

The remaining half is paid at completion, following the agreed walkthrough and completion requirements.

We can discuss other payment arrangements. Larger builds may use progress payments tied to completed work or milestones rather than a 50/50 split. Deposits, payment timing, changes, and completion requirements are set out in the signed agreement and must comply with applicable requirements.

How do we begin? What should I send you?

You don’t need to have anything prepared. We welcome a completely open conversation, even if you’re unsure what you want to build or where to start.

We’re a small, hands-on office. When you contact Marra, you speak directly with us—the people working on your project.

If you have them, a location, photos, drawings, or thoughts about budget and timing can help. They’re useful starting points, not requirements for saying hello.

Start a conversation ↗

What if I mainly need construction, rather than design?

That’s welcome too. We can work from existing plans, focus on a defined repair or renovation, or keep design involvement limited to the decisions your project actually needs.

We like being involved early because it helps connect the plans to the work on site. But you don’t need to commission a full design service to work with us.

Doesn’t “designer” just mean more expensive?

Design is a service with a fee, but it doesn’t have to mean expensive finishes or a more elaborate building. It can mean a simpler layout, better daylight, fewer awkward spaces, or deciding what not to build.

Housing is expensive enough that decisions about space, materials, comfort, and future use deserve careful thought. We see design as a way to make those decisions deliberately, within a budget—not as an excuse to disregard one.

We prefer to connect design and construction from the start, but we can scale our involvement to the project. We’ll explain the fee and deliverables before you commit. Design can help avoid some costly changes; it cannot guarantee a cheaper build.

How much does an ADU cost in the Salt Lake Valley?

ADU type, size, site conditions, and finishes all affect cost. A Salt Lake City builder’s published 2026 guide gives these broad planning ranges:

ADU type Planning range
Garage conversion$80,000–$150,000
Internal or basement ADU$90,000–$175,000
Attached ADU$150,000–$280,000
Detached new construction$175,000–$350,000+

These are market references, not Marra quotes or guaranteed total project budgets. We need to establish what is included, from design and permits to utility work and contingency. Your property’s access, slope, and existing services can matter more than its neighborhood.

Source: Ironwood Custom Builders’ 2026 Salt Lake City ADU guide. Referenced November 2026.

How long does an ADU take to construct?

A Salt Lake City builder’s published guide lists the following construction timelines:

ADU type Construction time
Garage conversion3–6 months
Internal or basement ADU4–7 months
Attached ADU5–9 months
Detached new construction6–12 months

Treat these as planning references, not promised completion dates. Allow additional time for design and permitting. Weather, utility work, inspections, material availability, and changes can affect your schedule.

Source: Ironwood Custom Builders’ 2026 Salt Lake City ADU guide. Referenced November 2026.
